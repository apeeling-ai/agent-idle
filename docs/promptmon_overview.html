Executive summary

An ambient companion that turns a developer's real work into a creature worth caring about.

Promptmon is a desktop pet for developers. A small creature lives on your second monitor and is fed by your actual Claude usage — prompt in Claude Code and it eats, and a thoughtful prompt feeds it better than a lazy one. Sustained, real output dresses it in visible armor and ranks it on a public leaderboard, so a strong engineer's high score is something genuinely earned, not claimed. It starts as one pet and grows into a small world built entirely from the things developers actually do.

The audience is developers, and the moat is recognition humor — every part of the game is a Tuesday someone actually had, which is exactly what makes it spread in dev communities. Anthropic itself shipped a terminal pet, pulled it, and the community revived it; our edge is not "a terminal pet" but the world around it: earned armor, cross-device presence (laptop and phone, one creature), and a leaderboard people can trust.

It ships in stages — a desktop pet that reacts to Claude Code and earns armor, then cross-device sync, then the public leaderboard with a one-tap shareable flex card, then the wider world of zones. Planning is complete: a full set of plans and a runnable foundation exist. The only thing left before building is a short team decision on a handful of sensible defaults.

01

The core loop

Two feeding rhythms, one creature — and the quality of your prompts is the quality of its food.

APassive — the idle soul

Your real Claude usage feeds it automatically. You mostly watch it thrive as you work; prompt in Claude Code and it eats on the other screen.

BActive — the Tamagotchi soul

Tap to feed, pet it, and send workers to zones. This is what makes the phone a full citizen, since a phone can't sense your usage.

Prompt quality is food quality: a lazy "make it pop" is junk food that leaves it Confused; a prompt with context and an example is a gourmet meal — and good prompting lifts your rank, not just your hunger bar.

03

The social layer

The viral engine — and it only works if people trust it.

★Leaderboards

Server-authoritative and ranked by a composite score that rewards good prompting and consistency, not raw volume. An Open tier for fun and a Verified tier that means something. Scopes by language, team, and friends, with weekly resets so newcomers can win.

⤴The share card

A one-tap, screenshot-ready image of your creature, its armor, and its rank — built to be posted. This is the loop that turns a private toy into something that spreads through dev Discords.

04

The world — Path B zones

A map of a developer's actual life. Each zone is a real thing devs do, rendered as a place.

🔥Bug Hell
Debugging. Bugs have HP = stack-trace length; Heisenbugs vanish when observed.
🌌Coding Matrix
Flow state. Output multiplies — until any interruption ejects you and breaks the streak.
☕Architecture Spa
System design. Blissfully relaxing; the spa water is secretly your Tech Debt rising.
⚔️Planning Playground
Sprint planning. Estimates are always wrong; the Stakeholder changes the brief.
🪦Post-Mortem Graveyard
Incident retros. Skip the blameless ritual and old bugs resurrect.
🌙On-Call Watchtower
Paging. Random night alerts can wake your creature. PagerDuty as a 3 AM rooster.
⚙️The Pipeline
CI/CD. Flaky tests fail at random; a red build halts the whole island.
🔥Merge Conflict Coliseum
Rebasing. Fight <<< HEAD monsters; a fumbled force-push wipes the day.
🕳️Dependency Dungeon
Packages. Treasure and supply-chain traps; a CVE wraith the night before release.
🪜Tutorial Hell
Learning. An Escher staircase of todo-app tutorials you can never escape.
🫧The Meeting Mire
Standups. A swamp that eats your afternoon. "This could've been an email."
🕸️Tech Debt Swamp
Systemic. Every shortcut sinks here; if it floods, the whole map slows down.
