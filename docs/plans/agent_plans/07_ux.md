# Agent 07 — User Experience (UX)

> Greenfield. Owns how it *feels*. **Open decision D1 (failure state)** is primarily a
> UX/tone call — this agent prototypes both and frames the choice (§6).

---

## 1. Mandate

Design the relationship between a person and their creature: the two feeding rhythms,
the first-minute hook, the ambient second-monitor presence, the emotional pull of
neglect, and the share moment. Make passive and active feel like one bond, not two
modes.

## 2. The two rhythms, one creature

- **Passive (idle-game soul):** the creature eats from your *real* Claude usage. You
  mostly watch it thrive while you work. This is the ambient magic.
- **Active (Tamagotchi soul):** you tap to feed, pet, and (later) assign workers to
  zones. This is what makes the phone a full citizen even though it can't sense usage.

UX rule: the creature should react the *same way* whether a feed came from a hook or a
tap. The person feels one relationship; the source is invisible.

## 3. The first minute (onboarding)

The hook is the moment "it ate *because I prompted*." Engineer onboarding to reach it
fast: hatch → name the creature (the "soul") → trigger one reaction. On desktop, the
first real Claude Code prompt that feeds it is the payoff; stage the install + hook
registration so that moment lands within the first session, not after configuration
fatigue.

## 4. Ambient, not nagging

It lives on a second monitor, so it must be **glanceable and never demand focus**:
- No modal popups, no focus stealing, no sound by default (the reference pet was built
  precisely for "can't use sound at the office").
- Mood reads at a glance via posture/expression — you should understand its state in a
  half-second peripheral glance.
- Notifications are gentle and rate-limited ("getting peckish"), respect Do-Not-
  Disturb, and never guilt-trip.

This restraint is also an anti-pattern guard: we are explicitly *not* building an
engagement-maximizing dark-pattern pet. The creature asks for care; it doesn't nag for
retention.

## 5. Mood & state map

Define a clear, legible state machine the renderer animates:
`in_the_zone → content → peckish → desperate → (faint | death)`, plus transient
`confused` (after junk-food prompts) and later `striking` (PPTemple). Each state has a
distinct, instantly-readable pose and a short, in-character line. Keep the vocabulary
small so it's learnable.

## 6. The failure state (D1) — UX owns the framing

Prototype **both** and present the tradeoff for the team to decide:
- **Faint & recover (forgiving):** neglect → faints → recovers on feeding. Broad
  appeal, low anxiety, friendlier to casual/forgetful users. Risk: lower stakes, less
  emotional weight.
- **Permanent death (hardcore Tamagotchi):** prolonged neglect → the creature dies, a
  small funeral, start over. High emotional stakes, strong word-of-mouth ("my guy
  died"), but punishing and potentially alienating; interacts with streak loss
  (Progression) and could feel cruel tied to *work* habits.

UX recommendation to inform the call: consider death as an **opt-in hardcore mode**
rather than the default, so the forgiving experience is the on-ramp and stakes are a
choice. Final decision is the team's (D1).

## 7. Interaction design details

- **Desktop:** click the creature to feed/pet; right-click for a small menu (themes,
  settings, the worker/zone panel later). Respect window passthrough so empty pixels
  don't steal clicks (Frontend handles the mechanism).
- **Mobile:** touch-first — big tap targets, the share card one thumb away, no hover
  dependencies.
- **The share moment:** turning pride into a post must be one obvious tap from seeing
  your creature/rank. Design this path as a first-class flow, not a buried button
  (with Social + Frontend).

## 8. Accessibility & comfort

- Colorblind-safe mood cues (don't encode state in color alone — use posture/icon).
- Reduced-motion option (matters for an always-on-screen animation).
- Quiet hours / DND honoring.
- Clear, plain-language consent for the sensor and for leaderboard visibility (trust
  is UX, too).

## 9. Interfaces & handoffs

- **Consumes:** Engine's state/mood outputs; Progression's reward pacing.
- **Produces:** the interaction model, the mood/state map, onboarding flow,
  notification rules, the neglect/failure experiences (both), the share-moment UX.
- **Drives:** Frontend (what to build), Social (the share moment), Progression
  (reward timing must *feel* right).

## 10. Risks

- **Nagging/dark-pattern drift** → the ambient, non-nagging rule (§4) is a hard line.
- **Onboarding drop-off before the hook** → ruthlessly shorten time-to-first-reaction.
- **Cruelty of permanent death tied to work** → the opt-in-hardcore recommendation.
- **Cluttered second-monitor presence** → glanceability budget; minimal chrome.

## 11. Open decisions surfaced

- **D1** failure state (UX frames; team decides) — recommend opt-in hardcore.
- Notification frequency ceiling (tune in playtest).

## 12. Definition of done

A documented interaction model + mood map; both failure experiences prototyped;
onboarding reaches the first reaction inside one session; the share moment is a single
obvious tap; comfort/accessibility rules specified for Frontend.

## 13. First three tasks

1. Map the mood/state machine and write each state's pose + line.
2. Prototype both failure experiences as clickable flows for the D1 decision.
3. Design the time-to-first-reaction onboarding and the one-tap share moment.
